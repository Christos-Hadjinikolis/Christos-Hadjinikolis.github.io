2. What are 1-2 examples of how you have impacted the success of your team/department/organisation over the past 6 months?

The 1st of December 2023 marks three years since I joined Vortexa. I want to take this opportunity to elaborate on my experience over these years and talk about my overall impact and achievements. As I reflect on my three-year journey with Vortexa, beginning on December 1, 2020, it's evident how my role has evolved alongside the company's growth trajectory.

Transformative Contributions and Technical Leadership at Vortexa
My arrival coincided with a significant milestone, the closing of our Series B funding round, a period that was not only about capital infusion but also about expanding our team's capabilities. Even before officially joining, I was actively involved in the recruitment process, assessing and interviewing potential engineering candidates. This early involvement allowed me to help shape the team that would drive our next phase of development.

During this critical transition from a startup to a scale-up, I tackled the pressing challenge of technical debt as a newly appointed Machine Learning (ML) Engineer. My responsibilities were diverse and impactful. I spearheaded initiatives to fortify our production pipeline, focusing on areas like enhancing unit-testing coverage and streamlining the development experience. By building end-to-end tests with characteristic sample data, I enabled a 'shift-to-the-left' approach in our engineering processes, allowing for faster feedback loops and significantly reducing the time to production.

Furthermore, I addressed the need for better tooling and engineering practices by optimizing the use of tools and replacing inefficient hacks. For instance, I replaced the use of vendor packages in our GitHub repositories, which were crucial for sharing common utilities across projects. This not only improved our code quality but also facilitated smoother collaboration among teams.
Recognizing a gap in our approach to ML operations, I embarked on a mission to cultivate a culture of MLOps within Vortexa. I took it upon myself to educate our engineers and data scientists about the nuances of ML engineering, covering essential topics such as model versioning, data-model dependencies, and model monitoring. This initiative was more than just sharing knowledge; it was about transforming our approach to ML engineering, ensuring that our practices were robust, scalable, and aligned with the industry standards.

In the initial phase of my tenure, I focused on the Vessel-state repository, a crucial component of our production pipeline. Initially, the repository's progress was bottlenecked by its dependency on Gustavo's reviews. To address this, I initiated a comprehensive restructuring of the repository to mirror its directed acyclic graph (DAG) structure more intuitively. This effort, combined with my initiative to write the first unit tests, laid the foundation for a more scalable and efficient workflow. Later in that first year,  I was appointed as a pod lead for Turingeries, and I want to believe that my approach to mentorship played a significant role in this transformation. By guiding and nurturing the younger members of my team, we collectively enhanced the test coverage of the repository to over 40% within a year. This achievement was accompanied by a significant refactoring that optimized our processes and codebase.

Another major project was the productionisation of Gustavo's work on voyages generation, later known as 'bon-voyage.' Here, I identified and addressed a critical bottleneck in our I/O processes across Python and Airflow repositories. The existing I/O management was inconsistent and often intertwined with business logic, leading to codebases lacking in clarity and ease of use. To resolve this, I developed 'Dynamicio,' a library that contributed to revolutionising our approach to managing internal libraries, which until then relied on inefficient GitHub vendor packages. This innovation was pivotal in introducing AWS code-artifact, fostering the publication of numerous Python packages (e.g. vtx-runners, graph-structure) as independent libraries and marking a new era for the company’s Python teams. I delivered Bon-voyage and voyages successfully, regularly interacting with ADT and constantly exposing myself to our full production pipeline and its intricacies,  leading the voyages destination updating in Autobahn (a stream-processing repo written in java/kotlin and using an up to then unknown framework to me, kafka-streams) implementation, and implementing monitoring in Bon voyage and autobahn, as well as contributing to the discovery and resolution numerous bugs.

The development and evolution of Dynamicio within Bon-voyage, and its further establishment as a key component in over 15 repositories, serve as a testament to my impactful contributions to our software ecosystem. This achievement was not just a technical feat but also a reflection of my principled approach towards software development. I religiously prioritized maintaining consistency across systems, ensuring that similar frameworks and tooling were utilized cohesively across different projects. This philosophy was crucial in driving the initiatives forward.
A highlight of this approach was the extensive three-month refactoring of the Vessel-state repository. This process, paired with the implementation of local end-to-end testing using sample data, exemplifies my commitment to systematic and reliable development practices. By streamlining the development process within the repository, we significantly reduced reliance on Gustavo’s oversight. More importantly, this initiative instilled confidence in developers of all levels, enabling them to contribute more effectively and with greater assurance. This not only enhanced productivity but also minimized the risk of unintended disruptions in our production pipeline, reinforcing the stability and robustness of our systems.

My growing exposure to a substantial amount of production code at Vortexa sparked a deep curiosity about the company's development journey. To satisfy this curiosity and share insights with the team, I embarked on a project to unravel our development history. I conducted interviews with key engineers and contributors, piecing together a data flow graph that illustrated the evolution of our main processing components. This research culminated in a comprehensive presentation to the entire company. My goal was to educate everyone about our developmental lineage and to initiate discussions on how we could further enhance and evolve our systems as the company continues to grow.

During this period, I recognized the immense value of documenting architectural decisions. I became a proponent of Architectural Decision Records (ADRs), advocating for their use as a vital tool for both new and current developers to trace the evolution of our systems and the rationale behind our decisions in response to changing business requirements. Concurrently, I initiated the 'system-design meeting,' which later evolved into the 'architecture forum' catch-ups. These forums were aimed at fostering a communal and inclusive approach to system design, a principle I passionately support.
Despite my initial opposition to the idea of appointing a dedicated architect, I was soon considered for a role in the company’s Architecture Guild. In this new capacity, I endeavoured to cultivate a fresh culture towards system design. My aim was to educate colleagues and facilitate the sharing of opinions through respectful discourse, aligning with the philosophies of renowned software engineer Martin Fowler. While the initiative faced its challenges, with some of the most critical feedback coming from my own reflections, I believe it was instrumental in pushing us to evolve and consider more collaborative and effective ways to engage with fellow developers.

As Vortexa underwent a significant structural transition with the merger of PMMT and SPET into DPT, I was entrusted with the responsibility of delivering the new destination model, a challenge I eagerly embraced. This task involved taking over 'Homework,' a legacy repository characterized by poor documentation and a history of suboptimal management. The repository was riddled with patchwork and a lack of clarity in its internal functions, which were dense with specialized business logic and other complexities. Navigating this landscape was far from straightforward.

Despite accurately anticipating many of the challenges, our initial efforts fell short of meeting the set deadlines. However, we persevered and ultimately succeeded in delivering a groundbreaking solution: exposing the destination-model as a service. 'Ithaca,' the new model service, was a pioneering project in our infrastructure. It not only introduced novel ways of interacting with ML but also significantly streamlined various aspects of our production pipeline. This advancement resulted in enhancements in processing speed, latency, and throughput, marking a notable achievement in our technological capabilities.
Throughout this project, my role extended beyond technical leadership. I was simultaneously managing a fully remote team, including two new members who required comprehensive onboarding and mentoring. Balancing the intricacies of the project with the dynamics of team management, especially in a remote setting, was a testament to my adaptability and commitment to nurturing talent while steering complex initiatives to success.

In the last three months, my team and I have achieved a significant milestone by successfully delivering 100% of our Objectives and Key Results (OKRs). This accomplishment is not just a measure of meeting targets but a reflection of our substantial contributions to resolving critical challenges within the company. We have effectively addressed issues with LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas) predictions, which were previously a concern due to their inaccuracy. Additionally, we tackled the spikes in the waiting-time model for vessel arrival predictions, by releasing a new model, effectively enhancing the overall predictive reliability of waiting times.

One of our key initiatives involved implementing rules to control sanctioned destinations, thereby aligning with regulatory requirements and enhancing operational compliance. Concurrently, we devoted attention to reducing engineering technical debt. A notable outcome of this effort was the deployment of the waiting-time model as another service, henceforth called 'Chronos,' designed to address significant latency issues in predictions. This innovation not only solves existing problems concerned with arrival spikes in our predictions but also streamlines the complex 'Big-bang' process, which is crucial for retrospectively generating years of vessel-destination predictions.

These accomplishments represent not just the fulfilment of our immediate objectives but also a substantial leap forward in enhancing our operational efficiency and technical prowess. By delivering on these complex projects, we have set new standards in predictive accuracy and system optimization, further solidifying our position as a leading force in our industry.
