# Fit Assessment

## Overall View

This is a credible stretch role rather than a clean direct match. With Promet added to the evidence base, the fit is stronger on applied LLM systems engineering, but the role still asks for deeper frontier LLM customisation and alignment experience than the current evidence supports.

You look strong on staff-level ML systems engineering, production inference infrastructure, platform reliability, distributed pipelines, evaluation discipline, and technical leadership. Promet adds a credible story around the harness layer for custom LLM products: runtime abstraction, prompt/session orchestration, streaming, tool execution, permission boundaries, traceability, and deterministic verification loops. You look weaker on the parts of the brief that expect direct, hands-on ownership of frontier LLM customisation and alignment techniques at the level Apple describes.

## Why The Fit Is Strong

- You have real staff-level ownership of production ML systems rather than only modelling or notebook work.
- Your background clearly covers the gap between experimentation and deployment, which is central to this role.
- The CV shows distributed systems, streaming infrastructure, inference services, observability, CI/CD, and model lifecycle discipline.
- Promet shows applied LLM systems thinking beyond prompt writing: model-runtime adapters, tool governance, local-first configuration, traceability, and separation between model runtime, memory/RAG, tools, and orchestration.
- You have credible cross-functional leadership across ML, DS, and DE, which matches the seniority and influence expected here.
- The PhD and research background support the "researcher plus engineer" side of the role, even though the job is not purely research.
- Python and Java are present in the profile, and the overall systems orientation is much closer to Apple's needs than a generic data scientist CV would be.

## Where The Fit Is Partial Or Weak

- The strongest gap is direct evidence of LLM-specific fine-tuning and alignment work.
- The job explicitly asks for RLHF/RLAIF, LoRA/QLoRA, prompt optimisation, and evaluation frameworks for large language models; Promet improves the prompt/orchestration/evaluation-adjacent story, but does not prove deep alignment or fine-tuning experience.
- Swift is requested and is not present in the current CV.
- The role mentions pre-training or continued pre-training, quantisation, speculative decoding, KV-cache optimisation, and hardware-aware model compilation; those are not currently evidenced in the source CV.
- Agentic frameworks and multimodal AI systems are listed as preferred qualifications. Promet supports tool-using agent architecture, but does not claim production use of named frameworks such as LangGraph, DSPy, or AutoGen.
- The role emphasizes standalone AI-native products. Promet helps here as a reference portfolio project, but it should not be represented as a commercial production product.

## Why The Variant Was Still Worth Creating

The role still overlaps substantially with your strongest areas:

- productionisation of intelligent systems
- distributed ML infrastructure
- evaluation and observability discipline
- applied LLM product infrastructure through Promet
- technical leadership
- end-to-end system ownership

That makes the role plausible enough to target, provided the CV is positioned honestly as a senior ML systems and platform engineer with applied LLM systems experience, not as someone already proven in every frontier LLM specialism listed.

## Tailoring Choices Made In The Variant

- Rewrote the summary to emphasise production-grade AI systems, platform delivery, transformer-based work, and ownership from experimentation to deployment.
- Updated the summary to include Promet and to explicitly frame the fit around the systems layer of generative AI: runtime boundaries, orchestration, tools, traceability, and verification.
- Strengthened Vortexa bullets around model-serving systems, distributed data flows, lifecycle engineering, reliability, and technical leadership.
- Tightened the skills sidebar to foreground technologies, LLM harnesses, tool safety, Ollama, and production concerns that are closer to the Apple brief.
- Kept the language honest and avoided claims about RLHF, LoRA, QLoRA, Swift, or pre-training.

## Claims Intentionally Not Made

- No claim of hands-on RLHF or RLAIF experience.
- No claim of LoRA, QLoRA, DPO, or PPO implementation work.
- No claim of Swift development.
- No claim of pre-training or continued pre-training of LLMs.
- No claim of multimodal or agentic-framework delivery where the current source CV does not support it.
- No claim that Promet is a commercial production product.
- No claim of inference-optimisation techniques like GPTQ, AWQ, speculative decoding, or KV-cache optimisation.

## Recommendation

You are a better fit if the hiring team values:

- staff-level ML systems engineering
- production reliability
- platform maturity
- transformer-adjacent system delivery
- applied LLM harness/product infrastructure
- technical leadership across research-to-production boundaries

You are a weaker fit if they are specifically screening for a candidate who has already spent significant time shipping LLM alignment and fine-tuning systems with named methods like RLHF, LoRA, or continued pre-training.

In practical terms: worth applying if you want the stretch, but the CV should be complemented by a strong cover note or conversation that explains your transition from production ML systems into generative AI platform work and makes Promet a concrete discussion point.
